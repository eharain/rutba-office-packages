# Chinese (Simplified) glossary for Rutba Office

The words the Simplified Chinese pack (`lang-zh-hans`, region zh-CN) uses for
the suite's recurring terms, so the same thing is called the same everywhere.
It follows the words people in mainland China already know from Microsoft
Office and Windows in Simplified Chinese where those have a word (文件, 开始,
插入, 另存为, 撤消, 批注, 修订), and plain mainland usage where they do not
(软件, 文件夹, 网址).

**This glossary was drafted by machine and needs a native speaker's review**
before the pack is called finished. A reviewer changes a word here first, then
in the pack, so the two stay in step.

## How the pack is written

- **Commands** are a bare verb or verb phrase, as Office does; no 请, no
  imperative particle: 保存 (Save), 打开 (Open), 关闭 (Close), 插入 (Insert),
  另存为… (Save As…). A command that opens a dialog keeps the ellipsis … at
  its end: 浏览…, 更改…, 其他颜色….
- **The reader** is addressed as 你, as current Microsoft products in zh-CN
  do, not 您: 你的日历已有新的详细信息。 Where a sentence reads naturally with
  no pronoun, leave it out: 已添加到日历，答复已准备好发送。
- **Tab and group names** are the Office nouns or short verb phrases: 文件,
  开始, 插入, 设计, 布局, 审阅, 视图; groups such as 剪贴板, 字体, 段落, 样式.
  Keep ribbon labels to two to four characters wherever Office does.
- **Placeholders** such as `{name}`, `{count}`, `{date}` stay exactly as
  written, in braces, untranslated. Chinese word order moves them where the
  sentence wants them: Unpin {name} → 取消固定 {name}; Week of {date} →
  {date} 所在的周; {name} accepted. → {name} 已接受。 Chinese has no case, gender
  or number agreement, so `{name}` never changes the words round it. For a
  possessive use 的: {name} 的更多选项.
- **Spacing**: put an ordinary half-width space between Chinese and Latin
  letters, digits or a placeholder, as Windows and Office do (Microsoft 帐户,
  第 {n} 天，共 {count} 天, 打开 YouTube Studio); no space next to full-width
  punctuation (保存：{name}).
- **Keys and shortcuts** stay as they are: 粗体 (Ctrl+B), 新建事件 (Ctrl+N),
  F7, Shift+F2.
- **File extensions, formats and codes** stay as they are: .docx, .ics, PDF,
  CSV, JSON, RTMP, H.264, AAC, SQL, HTTPS, `=SUM(ABOVE)`, `dd/mm/yyyy`.
- **Brand and product names** stay in Latin letters: Rutba Office, Rutba,
  Microsoft, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, Windows, Google, Gmail, iCloud,
  YouTube Studio, LinkedIn, TikTok, Power Query, SmartArt. Office's own
  translated feature names are used where Office translates them (艺术字 for
  WordArt, 数据透视表 for PivotTable).
- **The app names** are translated (see the first table); they read as plain
  nouns: 在“联系人”中, 用“邮件”打开. When an app name sits inside a sentence and
  could be read as an ordinary word, put it in quotation marks: 在“邮件”的
  “帐户”下添加一个。
- **Punctuation** is full-width in Chinese text: ，。：；？！（）. Quotation
  marks are “ ” with ‘ ’ inside them; titles of works take 《 》. The list
  comma 、 separates items in a series: 学生、课程、班级、出勤、成绩和费用。
  Labels that end without a full stop in English (buttons, menu items, tab
  names) end without one in Chinese too.
- **No em dash.** Chinese would use (two long dashes), but the pack does not. Where the
  English has (long dash) , write a full-width colon, a comma, parentheses or a new
  sentence: Banded Rows (long dash) every other row shaded → 镶边行：隔行添加底纹;
  B5 (long dash) 176 × 250 mm → B5（176 × 250 mm）. An en dash in a range (1 to 10) is
  fine.
- **No no-break spaces** (U+00A0, U+202F, U+2007): ordinary spaces only.
- **Counts**: Chinese has one plural form, `other`, so every counted label is
  one line. The noun does not change; it takes the right measure word after
  the number: 张 for slides and pictures, 页 for pages, 封 for mail, 个 for
  events, files and most things, 项 for changes and items, 位 or 人 for people,
  条 for records. One example:
  `other: {count} 张幻灯片` (1 张幻灯片, 5 张幻灯片, 1000 张幻灯片).
  Other counted labels: {count} 个事件, {count} 封邮件, {count} 位受邀者,
  {count} 项更改.
- **Numbers and dates** are formatted by the window from the region
  (zh-CN: 2026/11/12, 12:30); labels never spell out a format and never
  write numbers in Chinese characters. Write ordinals as 第 {n} 个. A date
  example in running text keeps its meaning, not its English form: "free on
  12 March?" → “3月12日有空吗？”.
- **Mainland words**, not Taiwan's: 软件, 文件, 文件夹, 保存, 打印, 默认,
  信息, 网络, 视频, 帐户 (as Windows writes it), 屏幕.
- **Same Chinese, two meanings**: Office itself uses 邮件 for both the Mail
  app and the Mailings tab, 公式 for both Formula and Equation, and 标题 for
  both Title and Heading (heading styles are 标题 1, 标题 2). The pack does
  the same; context separates them.

## Errors and plain sentences

Error messages, tooltips and explanations are full sentences: translate the
meaning in plain, natural Chinese, not word for word, and split a long English
sentence where Chinese reads better as two. Keep a sentence's facts (a number,
a name in `{braces}`, a key, an address) exactly. Prefer 无法打开此文件。 over a
literal rendering, and 未发送：{reason} for "Not sent: {reason}". A sentence
ends with 。, a question with ？.

## Intents and versions

### Voice
- The reader is 你, as this glossary sets, and the pronoun is left out
  wherever the sentence reads without it, which is most labels.
- Chinese verbs have no imperative or infinitive form: a command is the bare
  verb or verb plus object (保存, 查找收件人). A button or menu item never takes
  请; a sentence takes it only for a request the reader may refuse.
- A state takes 正在 while it runs and 已 when it is done; a yes or no
  question takes 是否 in medium and 是否要 in complete.

### Short, medium and complete
- **Width**: each Chinese character and each full-width mark (，。：；？！“”（）、)
  takes 2 columns; a Latin letter, digit or half-width space takes 1; each
  `{placeholder}` takes 8. So Chinese is short in characters but not in
  columns: 查找收件人 is 10 columns, Find Recipient 14.
- **Short** keeps the verb first and the core noun after it. Drop, in this
  order: modifiers and 的 phrases; the particles 了, 吗, 呢, 吧 (a question
  keeps ？); 一个, 一张, 这个 before a noun that is not being counted; 你的,
  此, 该 where the control shows the thing; the second clause or sentence.
  Keep 已 and 正在 (they carry the state) and 无法, 找不到, 不足, 失败 (they
  carry the error kind).
- **Measure words with a count** stay in every version. Short drops the noun
  after the measure word when the control shows what is counted:
  {count} 张幻灯片 → {count} 张. Never keep the noun and drop the measure word.
- **Abbreviations**: Chinese never abbreviates with a full stop. Allowed: the
  shorter Office term where Office has two (设置, 格式), and the Latin codes
  this glossary keeps (PDF, KB, Ctrl+N). Not allowed: clipped words Office
  does not use (演示 for 演示文稿, 幻片 for 幻灯片), English words in place of
  Chinese ones, half-width marks to save columns.
- **Complete** adds the object or place English adds (在列表中, 从联系人中),
  writes the whole sentence, and asks with 是否要. Complete may equal medium.

### Punctuation and marks
- **Ellipsis**: one … (U+2026) at the end of a command that opens a dialog
  and of a working state, as Microsoft writes it; not the six-dot …… of
  Chinese prose, not three full stops. With an access key it follows the key:
  另存为(&A)….
- **Colon, question and exclamation marks** are full-width ：？！ with no space
  before or after, even next to Latin text or a placeholder: 无法发送：{why}。
  Avoid ！ in messages.
- **Quotation marks** are “ ” with ‘ ’ inside, with no space inside or
  outside them.
- **Capitalisation**: Chinese has none. Latin names keep their own case
  (Rutba Office, PDF); nothing English-style is added.
- **Full stops**: a version whose English ends with a full stop ends with 。;
  one whose English does not (buttons, titles, fragment errors such as
  找不到该图片的文件) has none. Each version follows its own English.
- **Parentheses** are full-width （） around Chinese, half-width ( ) around a
  key or an access key.

### Access keys
- Chinese has no Latin letter to mark, so the key goes after the word as
  (&X), with the English letter, half-width parentheses and no space before:
  &Edit → 编辑(&E); &File → 文件(&F); &View → 视图(&V).
- Before an ellipsis, never after it: Save &As… → 另存为(&A)….
- Every version of a label keeps the same (&X); a label with no & in English
  gets none, and & never goes before a Chinese character.
