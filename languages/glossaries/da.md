# Danish glossary for Rutba Office

The words the Danish pack (`lang-da`, da-DK) uses for the suite's recurring
terms, so the same thing is called the same everywhere. It follows the words
people already know from Microsoft Office and Windows in Danish where those
exist, and LibreOffice or plain everyday Danish where they do not.

**This glossary was drafted by machine and needs a native speaker's review**
before it is called finished. A reviewer changes a word here first, then in
the pack, so the two stay in step.

## How the pack is written

- **Commands** take the imperative, as Danish Office does: Gem (Save), Åbn
  (Open), Luk (Close), Indsæt (Insert), Kopiér (Copy). Where Office writes an
  accent on the imperative of an -ere verb, so does the pack: Kopiér, Markér,
  Sortér, Eksportér, Importér, Acceptér, Formatér, Rotér, Gruppér, Justér,
  Kontrollér. Office's own forms without it stay as Office has them:
  Annuller, Opdater, Rediger, Filtrer, Minimer, Maksimer.
- **The reader is addressed as du**, informally, as current Microsoft
  software does: "Luk programmet først, og gem en kopi af filen, hvis du ikke
  er sikker." Never De.
- **Capitals**: Danish uses sentence case. Only the first word of a label,
  tab or button takes a capital, whatever the English does: Align Selected
  Objects is "Justér markerede objekter", Bring to Front is "Placer forrest".
  Names keep their capitals.
- **Tab and group names** are the nouns or the imperatives Office shows on
  its ribbon: Filer, Hjem, Indsæt, Design, Layout, Gennemse, Vis.
- **Placeholders** such as `{name}`, `{count}` and `{table}` stay exactly as
  written, in braces, untranslated; Danish word order moves them where Danish
  wants them: "Tabel: {name}", "{table} er sammenkædet".
- **Keys and shortcuts** stay as they are: Ctrl+S, F7, Shift+F2. "Tryk på
  Ctrl+N for at tilføje en begivenhed."
- **File extensions, formats and codes** stay as they are: .docx, .ics, PDF,
  CSV, JSON, SQL, RTMP, H.264, AAC, `=SUM(ABOVE)`, `dd/mm/yyyy`. Function
  names in formulas stay in English (SUM, VLOOKUP).
- **Brand and product names** stay as written: Rutba Office, Microsoft, Word,
  Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, Windows, Google, iCloud, YouTube, Facebook,
  Instagram, LinkedIn, TikTok, WordArt, SmartArt, Power Query.
- **The app names** are translated as the table below says, and keep a
  capital as names: "Personer fra Kontakter", "Tilføj en i Mail under Konti."
- **Punctuation**: Danish quotation marks ”…” (both the closing mark,
  U+201D): ”ledig den 12. marts?”. An English em dash becomes a colon, a
  comma, parentheses or a new sentence, never a dash with spaces: "Konti:
  en CalDAV- eller CardDAV-server ...", "B5 (176 × 250 mm)". An en dash in a
  range (1 to 10) is fine. The ellipsis … stays at the end of a command that
  opens a dialog: Gennemse…, Flere farver…. A comma goes before a subordinate
  clause only where it helps reading (the newer "startkomma" is not used).
  Compounds with a code or a name take a hyphen: PDF-fil, CalDAV-server,
  Rutba Office-konto.
- **Spaces**: ordinary spaces only, never a no-break space (not before %, not
  between a number and its unit): "Grå 25 %" is written with an ordinary
  space, or as Office writes it, "Grå 25%".
- **Counts** use Danish's two plural forms, `one` and `other`, each written
  out in full with `{count}`:
  `one: {count} ændring`, `other: {count} ændringer`;
  `one: {count} begivenhed`, `other: {count} begivenheder`;
  `one: {count} dias`, `other: {count} dias` (some nouns do not change).
  Danish uses the plural for 0: "0 ændringer".
- **Numbers and dates** are formatted by the window from the region (da-DK:
  decimal comma, full stop or space between thousands, dates as 12.03.2026 or
  12. marts 2026). Labels never spell out a format, and a date written as an
  example in a sentence uses the Danish form: "den 12. marts". Months and
  weekdays take a small letter.
- **Special to Danish**:
  - The definite article is a suffix (filen, tabellen, kontoen) and cannot be
    hung on a placeholder. Write "tabellen {name}" or "{name}" alone, never
    "{name}en".
  - The genitive -s cannot be added safely to a placeholder (a name ending in
    s takes an apostrophe). Say it another way: "Flere for {name}", "Om
    {name} online", "Kontoen for {name}".
  - `{name}` and similar placeholders have unknown gender (en or et word), so
    an adjective or participle should not have to agree with them. Put the
    placeholder after a colon or reword: "Sammenkædning fjernet: {name}"
    rather than "{name} er fjernet/fjernede".
  - Status words that agree with an English noun (accepted, declined) take
    the common-gender form when they stand alone: accepteret, afvist,
    foreløbig.
  - Record means two things: the Record tab and button is "Optag"; a record
    in a database or table is "post" (plural "poster").

## Errors and plain sentences

Error messages and tooltips are full sentences: translate the meaning, in
plain informal Danish addressed to du, not word for word. Keep a sentence's
facts (a number, a name in `{braces}`, a key) exactly. Prefer "Filen kunne
ikke åbnes." over a literal rendering, and "Ikke sendt: {reason}" for the
English "Not sent: {reason}".

## Intents and versions

### Voice
- Buttons, menu items and ribbon commands take the imperative, verb first,
  with the accent Office writes on -ér verbs: Gem, Søg efter modtager, Kopiér.
- Sentences address the reader as du, never De.
- Results and errors use the passive with "er" or the s-passive, which do not
  agree with a placeholder: Linket er kopieret. Filen kunne ikke åbnes.

### By intent
| Intent | How Danish phrases it | English | Danish |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| action on a button | Imperative, then the object without article | Find Recipient | Søg efter modtager |

| name | Noun phrase, sentence case, compound as one word | Horizontal text | Vandret tekst |

### Short, medium and complete
- Short keeps the imperative and drops the object and any qualifier; if room
  allows one noun, keep it after the verb. Never keep the object alone without
  its verb. A placeholder is never dropped.
- Allowed abbreviations are the standard ones, with a full stop and no space
  inside: f.eks., nr., s. (side), ca., inkl., min. and maks. Unit symbols take
  none: s, h, MB. Not allowed: made-up cuts (Indst., Dok.), a cut inside a
  compound, and any abbreviation on a button or menu item.
- A counted label keeps `{count}` and its noun whole in both forms and
  shortens by dropping the words around them. Only a noun with a standard
  abbreviation shortens, the same in `one` and `other`: `{count} s.`.
- Complete adds the object, in the definite form where it is a known thing,
  and the place, and becomes a full sentence where the English complete is
  one; a question takes "Vil du ...?". Tooltips and screen readers read
  complete.

Worked examples (English short | medium | complete, then Danish):
1. Find | Find Recipient | Find a recipient in the list
   Søg | Søg efter modtager | Søg efter en modtager på listen
2. Folder settings | Folder settings | Settings for this folder
   Mappeindstillinger | Mappeindstillinger | Indstillinger for denne mappe
3. Remove {name}? | Remove {name} from your contacts? | Do you want to remove {name} from your contacts?
   Fjern {name}? | Fjern {name} fra dine kontakter? | Vil du fjerne {name} fra dine kontakter?

### Punctuation and marks
- A command that opens a dialog ends in … (U+2026) with no space before it:
  Gennemse…, Flere farver…. A command that acts at once has none. A state
  while working ends in … too.
- Colon, question mark and exclamation mark follow the word with no space
  before them; one ordinary space follows a colon. Never a no-break space. A
  new label writes a percentage with an ordinary space, 25 %, as Dansk
  Sprognævn sets it; an Office label that writes 25% keeps Office's form.
- Quotation marks ”…” (U+201D at both ends), and ’…’ inside them.
- Sentence case for buttons, menu items, headings and titles: only the first
  word and names take a capital. A comma always closes a leading subordinate
  clause (Hvis du ikke gemmer, går ...); no startkomma.
- A full sentence ends in a full stop, errors included. A fragment follows its
  English version: a full stop if the English has one, none if not. Buttons,
  menu items, headings and titles never end in a full stop, and errors never
  end in an exclamation mark.

### Access keys
- `&` goes before a letter of the Danish word, never the English letter for
  its own sake: &Edit is &Rediger, &File is &Filer.
- Prefer the first letter. If it is taken in the same menu or dialog, take a
  clear consonant later in the word, or the first letter of the second word:
  Gem &som.
- Use æ, ø, å and an accented é only when no plain letter is free, and avoid
  letters with descenders (g, j, p, q, y) while another is free. Each letter
  once per menu or dialog.
- Never put `&` before a space, a placeholder or a mark. If short drops the
  word that carries it, move it to a letter of the word that stays.
