# Croatian glossary for Rutba Office

The words the Croatian pack uses for the suite's recurring terms, so the same
thing is called the same everywhere. It follows the words people already know
from Microsoft Office and Windows in Croatian (hr-HR) where those have a word,
and plain standard Croatian where they do not.

**This glossary was drafted by machine and needs a native speaker's review**
before it is called finished. A reviewer changes a word here first, then in
the pack, so the two stay in step.

## How the pack is written

- **Commands** (buttons, menu items, ribbon commands) take the short
  second-person singular imperative, as Croatian Office does: Spremi (Save),
  Otvori (Open), Zatvori (Close), Umetni (Insert), Poravnaj lijevo (Align
  Left). A command that opens a dialog keeps the ellipsis: Pregledaj…,
  Više boja….
- **The reader** is addressed formally, with the plural "vi" form and a
  lowercase vi/vaš, as current Croatian Office and Windows do in sentences:
  Odaberite sliku. Zatvorite taj program prije nastavka. Vaš odgovor je
  spremljen. Past participles addressed to the reader take the plural, which
  needs no gender: Jeste li sigurni?
- **Tab and group names** are nouns, as in Office: Polazno, Umetanje,
  Crtanje, Pregled, Prikaz; groups Međuspremnik, Font, Odlomak, Stilovi.
- **Capitals**: Croatian writes labels in sentence case, never English title
  case. Only the first word and names take a capital: Bring to Front is
  Postavi ispred, Align Selected Objects is Poravnaj odabrane objekte,
  Check Accessibility is Provjera pristupačnosti.
- **Placeholders** such as `{name}`, `{count}`, `{host}` stay exactly as
  written, in braces, untranslated, wherever Croatian word order puts them.
  A placeholder cannot take a case ending, so word the label so that it
  stands in the nominative, after a colon or after a noun that carries the
  case: Table: {name} is Tablica: {name}; There is a table called {name}
  already is Tablica pod nazivom {name} već postoji; Unpin {name} is
  Otkvači stavku {name}.
- **Gender of `{name}`** is unknown, and the Croatian past tense agrees with
  it (prihvatio / prihvatila). Use the present tense or a colon instead:
  {name} accepted is {name}: prihvaćeno, {name} declined is {name}:
  odbijeno. The same goes for `{user}`, `{organizer}` and similar.
- **Keys and shortcuts** stay as they are: Ctrl+S, F7, Shift+F2, Enter, Esc.
  Novi događaj (Ctrl+N).
- **File extensions, formats and codes** stay as they are: .docx, .ics, PDF,
  CSV, RTMP, H.264, `=SUM(ABOVE)`, `dd/mm/yyyy`. Give them a noun to carry
  the case rather than a case ending: Events from a .ics is Događaji iz
  datoteke .ics; save as PDF is spremi u formatu PDF.
- **Brand and product names** stay in Latin letters, unchanged: Rutba Office,
  Microsoft, Word, Excel, Windows, Google, iCloud, YouTube, LinkedIn. Here too
  a noun carries the case: u programu Excel, na platformi YouTube, u
  aplikaciji YouTube Studio.
- **The suite's app names** are translated as in the table below and keep the
  nominative after a noun: Add one in Mail is Dodajte ga u aplikaciji Pošta;
  People from Contacts is Osobe iz aplikacije Kontakti.
- **Quotation marks** are Croatian „…“ (U+201E, U+201C): such as „slobodno
  12. ožujka?“.
- **No em dash.** Where the English has one, write a colon, a comma,
  parentheses or a new sentence: Accounts (long dash) a CalDAV or CardDAV server
  becomes Računi: poslužitelj CalDAV ili CardDAV. An en dash in a range is
  fine (1 to 10); never an en dash with spaces round it.
- **Spaces**: ordinary spaces only, never no-break spaces. Percentages are
  written as the English label has them: Siva 25%.
- **Counts** use Croatian's three plural forms, `one`, `few` and `other`, and
  verbs and adjectives agree with each:
  `{ one: 'Odabran je {count} slajd', few: 'Odabrana su {count} slajda', other: 'Odabrano je {count} slajdova' }`
  (one: 1, 21, 31; few: 2 to 4, 22 to 24; other: 0, 5 to 20, 25 and so on).
  For people: {count} osoba, {count} osobe, {count} osoba.
- **Numbers and dates** are formatted by the window from the region (decimal
  comma, dates such as 12. 3. 2026.), so labels never spell out a format.
  Spell a number out as a word only where the English does. Croatian marks
  ordinals with a full stop (1. dan); where a placeholder is a number,
  prefer wording that needs no ordinal: Day {n} of {count} is Dan {n} od
  {count}.
- **Standard Croatian words**, not Serbian or Bosnian ones, and the terms
  Croatian Windows uses: računalo (computer), datoteka (file), mapa
  (folder), poslužitelj (server), zaslon (screen), izbornik (menu), postavke
  (settings), tjedan (week), lozinka (password), e-pošta (email).

## Errors and plain sentences

Error messages, notes and tooltips are full sentences: translate the meaning
in plain, polite Croatian, addressed with "vi", not word for word. Keep a
sentence's facts (a number, a name in `{braces}`, a key) exactly. Prefer
"Datoteku nije moguće otvoriti." over a literal rendering, and "Nije poslano:
{reason}" over a sentence that needs the gender of whoever did it.

## Product names

The owner, 13 Nov 2026: "Use local but the English names should stay".

Rutba's named products (Rutba Office, Talk, Locker, Data Files, Studio, Recorder, and the other apps this glossary's app table names) take the word the app table gives them as their local name. Where the table keeps the English (Talk | Talk), the name is the same either way.

- In a sentence (a medium or a complete that is a sentence or a phrase), write the local name with the English name beside it in brackets the first time the label names it, such as "Tresor (Locker)". A second mention in the same label takes the local name alone. Where the local name is the English, write it once.
- Where the name is a mark or a short label (a title bar, an app tile, a ribbon tab, any short version), write the English name alone.
- The suite's generic app names (Documents, Mail, Calendar, Contacts, Pictures, and the like) are words of the language, not marks: write them as the app table gives them, with no English beside them.
- Other companies' products and marks (Windows, PDF, YouTube, LinkedIn, Microsoft, Google, WordArt, SmartArt, QR) stay as written.

## Intents and versions

### Voice

Commands take the short second-person singular imperative: Spremi, Snimi,
Zatvori prozor. Sentences use the formal vi, with lower-case vi, vaš and the
plural imperative: Odaberite sliku. A past tense never agrees with `{name}`:
use the colon form ({name}: prihvaćeno) or a noun that carries the gender.

### Short, medium and complete

- **Short** keeps, in this order: the verb of an action, its object noun, and
  the key words of an error (Nije moguće, nije uspjelo, Nedostaje, nije). It
  drops, in this order: possessives (vaš, svoj), demonstratives (ovaj, taj),
  place and purpose phrases (u ovom dokumentu, da biste ...), adjectives, a
  second sentence.
- Short never drops a placeholder, a shortcut in brackets (Ctrl+S), the
  ellipsis of a command that opens a dialog, or a č, ć, đ, š or ž.
- **Abbreviations** only in short, only when the words still do not fit, and
  only these standard ones: br. (broj), str. (stranica), npr., tj., maks.,
  min. (minimum). Word abbreviations take a full stop; units and codes stay as
  symbols with none (MB, px, s, min for minutes). Never abbreviate a verb, an
  app name or a button's word.
- **A counted label** keeps `{count}` and all three forms (one, few, other) in
  every version. To shorten it, drop the verb or use the colon form, which
  needs no agreement and is the same text in each form: medium `Odabrano je
  {count} slajdova` (other) becomes short `Odabrano: {count}` in all three.
- **Complete** adds what the English complete adds: the object (Pronađi
  primatelja na popisu), the place, the reason, the next step. A message in
  complete is whole sentences, each with its full stop, and a question opens
  with Želite li. Tooltips and screen readers read complete, so it must make
  sense with nothing around it.
- Short is never wider than medium, nor medium than complete. The three may
  be the same text.

Worked examples (English short | medium | complete, then Croatian):

1. Find | Find Recipient | Find a recipient in the list
   Pronađi | Pronađi primatelja | Pronađi primatelja na popisu
2. Remove {name}? | Remove {name} from your contacts? | Do you want to remove {name} from your contacts?
   Ukloniti kontakt {name}? | Ukloniti kontakt {name} s popisa kontakata? | Želite li ukloniti kontakt {name} s popisa kontakata?
3. Camera not allowed. | The camera was not allowed. | The camera was not allowed. Allow it in your system's privacy settings.
   Kamera nije dopuštena. | Pristup kameri nije dopušten. | Pristup kameri nije dopušten. Dopustite ga u postavkama privatnosti sustava.

### Punctuation and marks

- **Ellipsis**: the single character …, with no space before it, at the end of
  a command that opens a dialog or asks for more (Pregledaj…, Više boja…) and
  of a working state (Spremanje…). Not on a command that acts at once. A
  version keeps the ellipsis if the medium has it.
- **Colon, question mark, exclamation mark, semicolon**: no space before, one
  ordinary space after. Never a no-break space anywhere in pack text.
- **Quotation marks**: „…“ (U+201E opening, U+201C closing), ‚…‘ inside a
  quotation. The English straight quotes round a placeholder become „{name}“.
  Do not use the »…« marks that Croatian print also allows.
- **Capitals**: sentence case on buttons, menu items, tabs, groups, headings
  and dialog titles: the first word and names only. After a colon, lower case
  unless a name follows.
- **Full stops**: an error, warning or message that is a sentence ends with a
  full stop, and so does every sentence in complete. A button, menu item, name,
  heading, tab or a status fragment without a verb takes none, as the English
  version does. Questions end with ?. Do not use exclamation marks. The stop
  after an ordinal or a day number (12. ožujka, 1. dan) is part of the number.
- **Percent**: as the English label writes it, joined: 25%.

### Access keys

- Put `&` before one letter of the Croatian word, one `&` per label: &Edit is
  &Uredi, Close Window is &Zatvori prozor. Latin script needs no (&X) form.
- Prefer the first letter of the first word, then a clear consonant in that
  word, then a letter of the next word.
- Never č, ć, đ, š or ž: on the Croatian keyboard they sit where other
  layouts have punctuation, so Alt with them does not carry over.
- Avoid i and l (hard to see underlined) and letters with descenders (g j p q
  y) when another letter is free. Each letter is unique within one menu or
  dialog, and a command keeps the same letter in every app where it can.
