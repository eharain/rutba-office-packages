# Dutch glossary for Rutba Office

The words the Dutch pack (`lang-nl`, nl-NL) uses for the suite's recurring
terms, so the same thing is called the same everywhere. It follows the words
people already know from Microsoft Office and Windows in Dutch where those
have a word, and plain everyday Dutch (as in LibreOffice) where they do not.

**This glossary was drafted by machine and needs a native speaker's review**
before it is called finished. A reviewer changes a word here first, then in
the pack, so the two stay in step.

## How the pack is written

- **Commands** (buttons, menu items, ribbon commands) take the infinitive, as
  Office does: Opslaan (Save), Openen (Open), Sluiten (Close), Invoegen
  (Insert), Naam wijzigen (Rename), Wijzigingen bijhouden (Track Changes).
- **Instructions in sentences** use the imperative in the je-form: "Sluit dat
  programma eerst." "Kies Maken, Live gaan en vervolgens Stream."
- **The reader** is addressed informally with je/jouw/jij, as current
  Microsoft Office and Windows do in Dutch, never u: "Je antwoord wordt voor
  dit bestand op deze computer bewaard." Use "je" as the possessive where it
  reads naturally ("je agenda"), "jouw" only for emphasis.
- **Capitals**: sentence case, as Dutch Office does. Only the first word and
  names take a capital: Opslaan als sjabloon, Nieuwe dia, Titels blokkeren,
  Alle wijzigingen accepteren. English Title Case is never copied.
- **Tab and group names** are the words Office uses: Bestand, Start,
  Invoegen, Ontwerpen, Indeling, Controleren, Beeld. A group is named with a
  noun where Office has one: Klembord, Lettertype, Alinea, Stijlen.
- **Placeholders** such as `{name}`, `{count}`, `{table}` stay exactly as
  written, in braces, untranslated, where Dutch word order puts them. Dutch
  sends the verb to the end of a subordinate clause, so a placeholder often
  moves: "Bij het lezen weet {host} dat deze database is geopend, en vanaf
  waar." / "{room} is geboekt voor {when}."
- **Keys and shortcuts** stay as they are: Ctrl+S, F7, Shift+F2, Ctrl+N
  ("Nieuwe gebeurtenis (Ctrl+N)").
- **File extensions, formats and codes** stay as they are: .docx, .ics, PDF,
  CSV, JSON, RTMP, H.264, AAC, SQL, `=SUM(ABOVE)`, `dd/mm/yyyy`.
- **Brand and product names** stay as written: Rutba Office, Rutba,
  Microsoft, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, Windows, Google, iCloud,
  YouTube Studio, LinkedIn Live Studio, Twitch, WhatsApp. Feature names that
  Office keeps in English stay English (WordArt, SmartArt, Power Query);
  ones Office translates are translated (PivotTable = Draaitabel, AutoSum =
  AutoSom).
- **App names** are translated as the table below says (Documenten,
  Werkbladen, Presentaties ...). In running text they keep their capital:
  "Mensen uit Contactpersonen".
- **Punctuation**: Dutch quotation marks are “ ” (curly double), with ‘ ’ for
  a quote inside a quote: zoals “vrij op 12 maart?”. The ellipsis … stays at
  the end of a command that opens a dialog: Meer kleuren…, Bladeren…. A
  question ends with ?, as in English.
- **No em dash.** Where the English has one, write a colon, a comma,
  parentheses or a new sentence: "Een groep heeft geen eigen opvulling of
  omtrek: maak een vorm erin op." / "Gestreepte rijen (om de andere rij
  gearceerd, zoals de stijl dat doet)". No spaced en dash either; an en dash
  in a range (1 to 10) is fine.
- **No no-break spaces**: ordinary spaces only, also before % or a unit.
- **Counts** use Dutch's two plural forms, `one` and `other`, each written
  out in full with `{count}`: `one: {count} dia`, `other: {count} dia's`;
  `one: {count} gebeurtenis in {name}.`, `other: {count} gebeurtenissen in
  {name}.`; `one: {count} persoon`, `other: {count} personen`.
- **Plural spelling**: words ending in a long vowel take 's with an
  apostrophe (dia's, foto's, video's, menu's); words in -e, -el, -en, -er
  take -s (tabellen is the exception: tabel, tabellen).
- **Numbers and dates** are formatted by the window from the region (decimal
  comma, thousands point, 12-3-2026). A label never spells out a format or
  writes a number itself where a placeholder carries it.
- **Compounds** are written as one word, as Dutch requires: Streamsleutel,
  Serveradres, Tekenkleur, Tabelontwerp; a hyphen where vowels would clash
  or with a code: Pagina-indeling, E-mailaccount, PDF-bestand, 3D-model.
  Keep ribbon labels short; prefer the shorter Office word.
- **Articles and `{name}`**: Dutch has de and het, but verbs and adjectives
  do not change for the gender of `{name}`. Avoid putting an article or an
  inflected adjective straight before a placeholder whose noun is unknown;
  use a colon or name the thing: "Tabel: {name}", "Het blad {name}".
- **Delete and Remove**: Office uses Verwijderen for both. Use Verwijderen;
  where both stand side by side, Remove is Weghalen (or "Verwijderen uit
  ..."), so the two buttons differ.

## Errors and plain sentences

Error messages and tooltips are full sentences: translate the meaning, in
plain Dutch with je, not word for word. Keep a sentence's facts (a number, a
name in `{braces}`, a key) exactly. Prefer "Dit bestand kan niet worden
geopend." or "Niet verzonden: {reason}" over a literal rendering. A short
label that states a result uses the past participle: Toegevoegd, Gewijzigd,
Geaccepteerd.

## Intents and versions

### Voice
- Buttons, menu items and ribbon commands take the infinitive, object first:
  Opslaan, Ontvanger zoeken. Never the imperative on a control.
- Sentences address the reader as je, never u, and give instructions in the
  je-form imperative: "Meld je aan bij Talk."
- Results use the past participle (Gekopieerd, Toegevoegd); errors are plain
  sentences with "kan niet" or "kon niet". Sentence case everywhere.

### Short, medium and complete
- Short keeps the infinitive and drops the object and any qualifier; if room
  allows one noun, keep it before the infinitive. Never keep the object alone
  without its verb. A placeholder is never dropped.
- Allowed abbreviations are the standard ones, with a full stop and no space
  inside: nr., blz., min. and max. (minimum, maximum), bijv., o.a. Unit
  symbols take none: s, min, h, MB. Not allowed: made-up cuts (Inst., Doc.),
  a cut inside a compound, and any abbreviation on a button or menu item.
- A counted label keeps `{count}` and its noun whole in both forms and
  shortens by dropping the words around them. Only a noun with a standard
  abbreviation shortens, the same in `one` and `other`: `{count} blz.`.
- Complete adds the object with its article and the place, and becomes a
  full sentence where the English complete is one; a question takes
  "Wil je ...?". Tooltips and screen readers read complete.

Worked examples (English short | medium | complete, then Dutch):
1. Find | Find Recipient | Find a recipient in the list
   Zoeken | Ontvanger zoeken | Een ontvanger in de lijst zoeken
2. Folder settings | Folder settings | Settings for this folder
   Mapinstellingen | Mapinstellingen | Instellingen voor deze map
3. Remove {name}? | Remove {name} from your contacts? | Do you want to remove {name} from your contacts?
   {name} verwijderen? | {name} uit je contactpersonen verwijderen? | Wil je {name} uit je contactpersonen verwijderen?

### Punctuation and marks
- A command that opens a dialog ends in … (U+2026) with no space before it:
  Bladeren…, Meer kleuren…. A command that acts at once has none. A state
  while working ends in … too.
- Colon, question mark and exclamation mark follow the word with no space
  before them; one ordinary space follows a colon. Never a no-break space. A
  percentage is written straight after the number, 25%, as CLDR and Office
  write it.
- Quotation marks “ ” (U+201C, U+201D), and ‘ ’ inside them.
- Sentence case for buttons, menu items, headings and titles: only the first
  word and names take a capital.
- A full sentence ends in a full stop, errors included. A fragment follows its
  English version: a full stop if the English has one, none if not. Buttons,
  menu items, headings and titles never end in a full stop, and errors never
  end in an exclamation mark.

### Access keys
- `&` goes before a letter of the Dutch word, never the English letter for
  its own sake: &Edit is &Bewerken, &File is &Bestand.
- Prefer the first letter. If it is taken in the same menu or dialog, take a
  clear consonant later in the word, or the first letter of the second word:
  Opslaan &als.
- Never split ij: the `&` does not go before the j of ij. Avoid letters with
  a diacritic (ë, é) and letters with descenders (g, j, p, q, y) while a plain
  letter is free. Each letter once per menu or dialog.
- Never put `&` before a space, a placeholder or a mark. If short drops the
  word that carries it, move it to a letter of the word that stays.
