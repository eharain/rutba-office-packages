# Lithuanian glossary for Rutba Office

The words the Lithuanian pack uses for the suite's recurring terms, so the
same thing is called the same everywhere. It follows the words people already
know from Microsoft Office and Windows in Lithuanian (lt-LT) where those have
a word, LibreOffice where Office has none, and plain everyday Lithuanian
otherwise.

**This glossary was drafted by machine and needs a native speaker's review**
before it is called finished. A reviewer changes a word here first, then in
the pack, so the two stay in step.

## How the pack is written

- **Commands** take the infinitive, as Lithuanian Office does: Įrašyti
  (Save), Atidaryti (Open), Uždaryti (Close), Įterpti (Insert), Naikinti
  (Delete). A command with an object keeps the infinitive and puts the object
  in the accusative: Įrašyti kaip šabloną, Eksportuoti vaizdo įrašą. Full
  instructions in tooltips and help text take the polite plural imperative:
  Paspauskite Ctrl+N, kad įtrauktumėte įvykį.
- **The reader** is addressed formally, with jūs, as current Office does, and
  jūs / jūsų are written in lower case inside a sentence: Jūsų kalendoriuje
  yra nauja informacija. Prefer impersonal wording where it reads naturally:
  Įrašyta, bet neišsiųsta.
- **Capitals**: sentence case everywhere, as Lithuanian Office does, even
  where the English capitalises every word: Align Selected Objects becomes
  Lygiuoti pažymėtus objektus; Table Design becomes Lentelės dizainas.
- **Tabs and groups** are named with a noun, as Office does: Pagrindinis,
  Įterpimas, Piešimas, Peržiūra, Rodinys. A group name is a noun or a noun
  phrase: Mainų sritis, Šriftas, Pastraipa.
- **Placeholders** such as `{name}`, `{count}` and `{table}` stay exactly as
  written, in braces, untranslated. A placeholder cannot take a case ending,
  so the wording keeps it in the nominative: put a noun before it in the
  needed case (failas {name}, lentelėje {table}) or use a colon
  (Pervardyti: {name}, Lentelė: {name}).
- **Gender of `{name}`**: the past tense of a Lithuanian verb does not change
  with gender, so {name} priėmė, {name} atmetė work for anyone. Avoid past
  participles that agree with {name} (pakviestas / pakviesta); use a verb or
  an impersonal form instead: Pakviesti: {names}.
- **Keys and shortcuts** stay as they are: Ctrl+S, F7, Shift+F2, Enter, Esc.
  Naujas įvykis (Ctrl+N).
- **File extensions, formats and codes** stay as they are: .docx, .ics, PDF,
  CSV, JSON, SQL, RTMP, H.264, AAC, `dd/mm/yyyy`, `=SUM(ABOVE)`. Function
  names stay in English: SUM, VLOOKUP.
- **Brand and product names** stay in Latin letters, undeclined, with a noun
  carrying the case where one is needed: Rutba Office, Microsoft, Word, Excel,
  PowerPoint, Outlook, Windows, Google, iCloud, YouTube, Instagram. Example:
  „YouTube Studio“ programoje pasirinkite Kurti.
- **The suite's app names** are translated as in the table below and written
  with a capital letter as names: Dokumentai, Paštas, Kalendorius. With a
  case they need, use the noun programa: programoje Paštas.
- **Punctuation**: Lithuanian quotation marks „…“ (low opening, high
  closing): pvz., „laisva kovo 12 d.?“. The ellipsis … stays at the end of a
  command that opens a dialog: Daugiau spalvų…. A comma always goes before
  kad, kai, nes, jei, kuris.
- **No em dash.** Where the English has one, write a colon, a comma,
  parentheses or a new sentence: "A group has no fill or outline of its own:
  format a shape inside it" becomes Grupė neturi savo užpildo ar kontūro:
  formatuokite joje esančią figūrą. No spaced en dash as a dash either; an en
  dash in a range (1 to 10) is fine.
- **No no-break spaces**: ordinary spaces only, also in 10 % style numbers
  and in abbreviations such as el. paštas.
- **Abbreviations** keep the full stop: el. paštas, min., maks., pvz.
- **Counts** use the four forms of `lt` in this order: `one` (1, 21, 31, not
  11), `few` (2 to 9, 22 to 29, not 12 to 19), `many` (fractions, 1,5) and
  `other` (0, 10 to 20, 30, 100 ...). `one` takes the nominative singular,
  `few` the nominative plural, `many` the genitive singular and `other` the
  genitive plural:
  `{ one: '{count} skaidrė', few: '{count} skaidrės', many: '{count} skaidrės', other: '{count} skaidrių' }`;
  for a masculine noun: `{count} įvykis`, `{count} įvykiai`, `{count} įvykio`,
  `{count} įvykių`. The verb and any adjective agree with the form too:
  Išsiųsta {count} žmogui / žmonėms / žmogaus / žmonių (dative or genitive as
  the sentence needs, in every form).
- **Numbers and dates** are formatted by the window from the region (decimal
  comma, 1 000 grouping, 2026-11-12 dates), so labels never spell out a
  format. Ordinal dates in prose use the Lithuanian pattern only where a label
  already writes a sample: kovo 12 d.
- **Other**: write ė, į, ų, ū, š, ž, č, ą, ę correctly, never as plain
  Latin letters. "You" in the English often becomes no pronoun at all, since
  the verb ending already shows the person.

## Errors and plain sentences

Error messages and tooltips are full sentences: translate the meaning, in
plain polite Lithuanian, not word for word. Keep a sentence's facts (a
number, a name in `{braces}`, a key) exactly. Prefer "Šio failo atidaryti
nepavyko" over a literal rendering, and "Neišsiųsta: {reason}" for a short
status with a reason.

## Intents and versions

### Voice

Buttons and menu items take the infinitive (Įrašyti, Rasti gavėją).
Instructions inside sentences take the polite plural imperative
(Paspauskite, pasirinkite). The reader is jūs, in lower case, often with no
pronoun at all; prefer impersonal forms (Nepavyko ..., Ieškoma ...) and never
a participle that agrees with `{name}`.

### Short, medium and complete

- Medium is the version the pack shows today. Write it first, then shorten
  and extend from it, keeping the same words for the same things.
- Short: the infinitive or the key noun goes first. Drop jūsų, šį, šis and
  phrases of place or manner (sąraše, diske, dabar), but keep the noun
  before a placeholder. Keep every placeholder, key name and code.
- Abbreviate only with a standard Lithuanian abbreviation, with its full
  stop: el., min., maks., psl., pvz., d., nr. Never invent one, never
  abbreviate a verb, an app or brand name, or any word in an error, question
  or warning.
- A counted label keeps all four forms (one, few, many, other). Short drops
  the verb beside the count and keeps `{count}` with the noun in each form:
  `one: {count} failas`, `few: {count} failai`, `many: {count} failo`,
  `other: {count} failų`. With a standard abbreviation the four forms may be
  the same text: `{count} psl.`
- Complete: add the object and where it goes, as the English complete does,
  and end a sentence with a full stop. Where the English complete asks "Do
  you want to ...?", use Ar norite + infinitive: Ar norite pašalinti kontaktą
  {name} iš kontaktų?
- Tooltips and screen readers read complete, so it must make sense alone.

### Punctuation and marks

- Ellipsis: the single character … with no space before it, on a command
  that opens a dialog or asks for more (Įrašyti kaip…, Daugiau spalvų…), and
  on a state while working (Ieškoma pasiūlymų…). Not on a command that acts
  at once.
- Colon, question mark and exclamation mark: no space before, one ordinary
  space after: Organizatorius: {name}. Never a no-break space anywhere, also
  not before % or inside an abbreviation.
- Quotation marks: „…“ (low opening, high closing). The English straight
  quotes become „…“, also around a placeholder: „{text}“.
- Capitals: sentence case on buttons, menu items, tabs, headings and window
  titles; only the first word and names take a capital.
- Full stops: an error, warning or message that is a sentence ends with a
  full stop, as the English does; a label the English leaves without one
  (Picture file is missing) stays without one. Buttons, menu items, names
  and headings take none. No exclamation marks in errors.
- No dash of any kind between words: a colon or a comma takes its place.

### Access keys

- `&` goes before a letter of the Lithuanian word: &Failas, &Redaguoti,
  Įrašyti &kaip…
- Prefer the first letter, but never ą, č, ę, ė, į, š, ų, ū or ž: on the
  Lithuanian keyboard they sit on the number row, so the key differs between
  layouts. Take the next plain letter instead: Į&terpti. If another item in
